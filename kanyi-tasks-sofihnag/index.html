EP02 “The Pot” — first draft

Assigned 28 Jul 2026 · Draft due: before Mon 3 Aug 2026
▶ Watch the latest pilot cut first This is the tone, pace and dryness the draft should sit in. Watch it twice before writing.

The assignment

  1. The Pot — main story. Flesh the beat sheet below into full scenes: dialogue, timing, the escalation of Jamie’s cowardice. It’s the story as first written — but you’re not locked to it. Think outside the box.
  2. Optional — a dating-app substory for Jamie. If it sparks something, weave a Hinge B-story through the episode: Jamie swiping, drafting messages, over-thinking — his performance instinct applied to dating. It should rhyme with the A-story: a man who can’t confront a pot also can’t send a risky first message. How it threads through the time-jumps is yours to invent.
  3. Try a few comedic scenes. Beyond the outline, pitch 2–3 extra comic scenes or alt-beats — in the script or as an appendix. Big swings welcome; we’d rather cut than never see them.

Beat sheet (the story as first written — not locked)

  • Open on Jamie’s door. He walks out smiling; we follow him into the kitchen. He whistles, happily lays out his ingredients — then the whistling stops as he sees: the pot. He frowns.
  • He calls out to Malik in the other room: “Hey Malik, did you put the pot on the stove?”
  • “Yeah I did,” Malik responds offscreen.
  • Jamie sighs, nods, packs up his ingredients, heads back to his room.
  • Time card (Spongebob-style): “One hour later.”
  • Jamie’s door again. He walks out smiling — not as widely. Kitchen: the pot is still there. He sighs, lays out ingredients and cutlery anyway, leaves them on the counter.
  • The music turns dramatic. Jamie slowly approaches the pot as if to take it off the stove — horror-style shot framing. He’s interrupted by…
  • Malik walks in: “What are you doing?” Jamie snaps back to the other side of the kitchen: “Nothing, just preparing to cook.”
  • “Oh? Cool.” Jamie looks down and misses that as Malik goes to the sink, the pot’s handle catches his shirt sleeve.
  • As Jamie looks up he catches Malik turning the pot so the handle faces the other way.
  • “Dammit” — Jamie, internal (V.O.).
  • Downcast, Jamie walks back to his room, shoulders sagging.
  • Time card: “30 mins later.”
  • Jamie emerges miserable. The pot is still there. He sighs passive-aggressively; camera pans to his growling stomach. He opens and closes cupboards loudly, waiting for Malik to appear. He doesn’t.
  • Jamie asks ChatGPT if he’s the asshole for wanting to call out a flatmate who monopolises the one pot they own. ChatGPT first notes they really should own more than one pot at their age, then agrees: sometimes confrontation is the way to stand your ground. (Exact wording open — make it funnier.)
  • Confident, Jamie storms into the living room. Malik is gaming in headphones. “Malik, I think it’s rude that—” The doorbell rings.
  • “Ah, my food’s here,” Malik replies.
  • Cut to Jamie’s stunned face.
  • Slow tracking shot from the living room to the kitchen, landing on an over-the-pot shot: it has been empty the entire time.

Guardrails

  • London flat. London texture — Zoe owns the final pass on this, but write toward it.
  • First names only. No surnames.
  • Jamie is anxious and performing, not sitcom-optimistic. Malik is Indian/South-East Asian, dry, unhurried — silence is part of his delivery. (“It’s a pot.” — that register.)
  • Hinge is the show’s app.
  • V.O. is allowed — it’s used in the pilot too.
  • The ending is yours — take the story wherever you think it should land.

Format & process

  • Format: scene-by-scene like the Writers Pack pilot script — scene headers, action lines, dialogue. Vertical short-form; keep scenes lean.
  • Length: match the pilot’s scale (roughly a 4–5 minute episode).
  • Process: draft 1 → one round of notes from Zoe → draft 2.
  • Questions beat questions mid-draft — ask early.
